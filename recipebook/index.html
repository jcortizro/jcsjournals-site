Carrot-Cabbage Coleslaw

The transition diet salad base. A rough combination salad.

Watch the full walkthrough

This recipe has its own video in Transition Diet 101, Module 5, under Menu Plan 1. The video will be watchable there. Coming soon.

Transition Diet 101 →
Menu plan
Plan 1Coming off animal productsSee facing page
Meal
Lunchbaked squash, and a toasted bread or tortilla if you want one
Timing
15 to 20 minafter your fruit meal

Ingredients

  • 3 cups carrots (about 1½ to 2 large carrots)
  • 3 cups cabbage (about ¼ to ⅓ head of cabbage; green or red okay)
  • 1 fresh lemon, juiced

Equipment

  • Knife
  • Cutting board
  • Salad bowl
  • Vegetable peeler (optional)
  • Food processor or blender (optional, for faster prep)

Dressing, pick one

  • Olive oil (to taste)
  • Vinegar-free dressing (to taste)
  • Blended nut or seed cream (cashews, peanuts, pumpkin seeds), to taste
  1. Prepare the carrots. Wash carrots thoroughly. Peel if desired. Cut each carrot in half, then lengthwise into quarters. Finely mince the carrot into small, thin pieces for easier chewing and digestion. Transfer to a salad bowl.
  2. Prepare the cabbage. Slice off about one-third of the head. Cut into thin strips lengthwise, then rotate 90 degrees to the left or right and make thin lengthwise cuts again. Add the cabbage to the bowl with the carrots.
  3. Add the lemon juice. Cut the lemon in half and squeeze the juice directly over the carrot and cabbage mixture.
  4. Mix thoroughly. Mix well using a spoon or clean hands, ensuring all vegetables are evenly coated in lemon juice. Allow the salad to sit briefly so the lemon juice can soften the vegetables.
  5. Serve. Serve immediately or let rest a few minutes for a softer texture. Optional additions like olive oil or a simple dressing can be added if desired, but the base recipe is complete as-is.

Make it yours

Full explainer video + free infographic · TD 101 Module 4, Vegetable Combining

Rougher
Shredded raw beet, or celery cut thick. Adding a leaf to a base this solid actually softens it, so reach for a root instead.
Gentler
Trade a cup of the cabbage for spinach, arugula or spring mix. Lightly steaming the cabbage also softens it.
Seasoning
One or two only. Celery, cucumber, onion, beets or bell pepper. A herb like dill or parsley counts separately.
  • This salad is considered a rough combination salad and appears as the first salad base in the transition menus of Arnold Ehret.
  • Carrots alone, cabbage alone, or a combination of both can be used.
  • If cabbage feels too aggressive, lightly steaming or choosing red cabbage may be better tolerated.

Kale and Spinach Minced Salad

A soft and rough combination salad, sitting halfway between the coleslaw and the soft salad.

Watch the full walkthrough

This recipe has its own video in Transition Diet 101, Module 5, under Menu Plan 2. The video will be watchable there. Coming soon.

Transition Diet 101 →
Menu plan
Plan 2Plant-based, still lots of starchSee facing page
Meal
Lunch or Dinnerthe vegetable sauté at lunch, and baked zucchini alfredo plus sweet potato fries at dinner
Timing
15 to 20 minafter your fruit meal

Ingredients

  • Fresh spinach (soft base), about 3 to 4 cups, minced
  • Kale (rough base), about 2 to 3 cups, minced
  • Carrot (rough base), 1 cup, minced
  • Celery (seasoning vegetable), 1 cup, minced
  • Roma tomato (seasoning vegetable), ¼ to ⅓ cup, minced
  • Optional: olives, black or green, vinegar-free and low salt
  • Optional: shredded raw beet, for a rougher variation

Equipment

  • Knife
  • Cutting board
  • Salad bowl
  • Optional: blender or food processor, for faster prep

Dressing, pick one

  • Blended nut or seed cream (cashews, peanuts, pumpkin seeds)
  • Olive oil with lemon, lime, or orange juice
  • Vinegar-free store-bought dressing
  1. Prepare the spinach. Wash spinach thoroughly. Gather into a tight bundle and slice into thin strips, then rotate 90 degrees to the left or right and mince further if desired. Transfer to a salad bowl.
  2. Prepare the kale. Remove thick stems if present. Gather leaves into a ball and finely slice, then mince. Add to the bowl with the spinach.
  3. Prepare the carrot. Wash carrot and peel if preferred. Cut the carrot in half widthwise at the middle point, then slice each half lengthwise so you have 4 pieces of carrot. Cut each piece lengthwise into thin strips, then mince into small pieces until you reach 1 cup. Add to the salad bowl.
  4. Prepare the celery. Trim dirty ends and remove leafy tops if desired. Slice stalks lengthwise into thin strips, then gather and mince finely until you reach 1 cup. Add to the bowl.
  5. Prepare the tomato. Slice tomato lengthwise into thin strips, rotate, and mince into small pieces. Measure ¼ to ⅓ cup and add to the salad.
  6. Dress the salad. Add dressing of choice. Citrus-based dressings work especially well for softening greens over time.
  7. Massage the kale (optional but recommended). With clean hands, firmly squeeze and massage the salad for 30 seconds to 1 minute. The kale leaves will darken slightly, soften, and become tender. This step dramatically improves the flavor and texture of raw kale. This step is always done after adding your salad dressing.
  8. Serve. Eat immediately or allow to sit briefly for further softening. This salad can be eaten with a spoon when finely minced.

Make it yours

Full explainer video + free infographic · TD 101 Module 4, Vegetable Combining

Rougher
Move to 1 part soft to 1 part rough, so 3 cups spinach and 3 cups kale and carrot. Romaine also counts as rough.
Gentler
Move to 2 parts soft to 1 part rough, so 4 cups spinach and 2 cups kale and carrot. Arugula, Boston lettuce or spring mix work in place of some kale.
Seasoning
Swap the tomato for cucumber, onion, beets or bell pepper. One or two, never the whole list.
  • Soft base: spinach. Rough base: kale and carrot. Seasoning vegetables: celery and tomato.
  • Recommended ratios for a halfway soft and rough salad. 2/3 soft to 1/3 rough means 4 cups soft base plus 2 cups rough base. 1 soft to 1 rough means 3 cups soft base plus 3 cups rough base.

Spira's Soft Salad, Hearty Variation

A more filling take on Professor Spira's classic soft salad, still easy to digest.

Watch the full walkthrough

This recipe has its own video in Transition Diet 101, Module 5, under Menu Plan 3. The video will be watchable there. Coming soon.

Transition Diet 101 →
Menu plan
Plan 3Mainly raw fruits and vegetablesSee facing page
Meal
DinnerSpira's jackfruit and greens stew
Timing
15 to 20 minafter your fruit meal

Ingredients

  • Soft base: spinach, 3 cups, washed and roughly chopped
  • Soft base: lettuce of choice (red leaf, green leaf, romaine, or butter lettuce), 3 cups, chopped
  • Roughage and broom element: celery, 1 cup, chopped into thicker pieces
  • Seasoning and texture: beet, 1 cup, peeled and thinly sliced
  • Seasoning and texture: tomato, ¼ to ⅓ cup, chopped
  • Mucus-lean addition: black or green pitted olives, optional, added to taste

Equipment

  • Knife
  • Cutting board
  • Salad bowl

Dressing, pick one

  • Lemon juice (to taste)
  • Olive oil (about 1 to 2 tbsp)
  • Green mineral salt (pinch)
  1. Prepare the soft base. Start with spinach. Roughly chop, not finely minced, to keep the salad more substantial. Add washed lettuce leaves, stacked and sliced into bite-sized pieces. Mix spinach and lettuce together in the salad bowl.
  2. Add the celery (roughage component). Chop celery into thicker chunks rather than fine pieces. Add generously. This provides fiber, hydration, and the broom effect while keeping the salad soft overall.
  3. Incorporate seasoning vegetables. Peel, grate, or thinly slice then chop the beet and add to the salad bowl. Slice tomato lengthwise into thin strips, rotate, and mince into small pieces. Measure ¼ to ⅓ cup and add to the salad bowl.
  4. Add olives (optional mucus-lean item). Finely slice black or green pitted olives and add sparingly, according to taste.
  5. Season the salad. Sprinkle a small pinch of green salicornia salt. Drizzle lightly with olive oil. Finish with fresh lemon juice.
  6. Toss and serve. Gently toss until evenly coated. Taste and adjust dressing and seasoning as needed.

Make it yours

Full explainer video + free infographic · TD 101 Module 4, Vegetable Combining

Rougher
Add cabbage, carrot or kale to the base. Romaine counts as rough too, so swapping your lettuce for it changes the salad more than it looks.
Gentler
Keep the base to spinach with arugula, Boston lettuce or spring mix, and leave the beet out.
Seasoning
One or two only. Celery, cucumber, tomato, onion or bell pepper. The olives are optional.
  • This salad is a variation of Professor Spira's classic soft salad, designed to be more filling while still easy to digest.
  • It stays aligned with the transition-diet principle of simplicity outlined by Arnold Ehret, while allowing flexibility in seasoning and texture.

Apple Lemonade

The base fruit juice template. Everything else in the fruit juice family builds on it.

Transition Diet 101 →
Menu plan
Building blockBase juice template. The ingredient formula the other fruit juices build on.See facing page
Meal
Any meal
Timing
2 hoursbefore a fruit or vegetable meal

Ingredients

  • 4 to 5 apples (washed; skin, seeds, and core removed)
  • 1 lemon (peeled; rind removed)
  • Optional: fresh ginger (small piece)
  • Optional: honey (to taste)

Equipment

  • Cold-press or centrifugal juicer
  • Knife
  • Cutting board
  • Pulp collector (juicer attachment)
  • Juice container or pitcher
  1. Prepare the lemon. Peel the lemon and discard the rind into the pulp collector. Cut the lemon into quarters lengthwise, then cut each quarter into smaller pieces suitable for juicing.
  2. Prepare the apples. Wash apples thoroughly. Remove the core and seeds by slicing around the center core, leaving a seedless apple pillar to discard. Slice the remaining apple flesh lengthwise, then rotate and make small, thin cuts. This helps the juicer process the apples more easily.
  3. Juice them. Run the lemon and apple pieces through the juicer, alternating between the two so the juicer keeps flowing and does not clog. If you are using ginger, chop it into very small pieces first to protect the machine.
  4. Serve. Pour into a container and drink immediately. Stir gently if it separates. Add honey to taste if you want it sweeter.
  • This is the base the other fruit juices are built on. The Gourmet Mixed Fruit Juice adds grapes, oranges, blackberries and banana to the same two steps.

Gourmet Mixed Fruit Juice

The apple lemonade base built out into a fuller, richer juice.

Watch the full walkthrough

This recipe has its own video in Transition Diet 101, Module 5. The video will be watchable there. Coming soon.

Transition Diet 101 →
Menu plan
Building blockFruit juice variation. A richer build on the apple lemonade base, not assigned to a menu slot.See facing page
Meal
Any meal
Timing
2 hoursbefore a fruit or vegetable meal

Ingredients

  • 3 apples (washed; skin, seeds, and core removed)
  • 1 lemon (peeled)
  • Grapes, about 1 lb or ½ kg, removed from stems
  • 1 to 2 oranges (peeled)
  • Blackberries (to taste)
  • 1 to 2 ripe bananas (very ripe preferred)
  • Optional: fresh ginger root, small piece, to taste

Equipment

  • Cold-press juicer (recommended)
  • Knife
  • Cutting board
  • Pulp collector (juicer attachment)
  • Large juice container or pitcher
  1. Prepare the lemon. Peel the lemon and discard the rind into the pulp collector. Cut the lemon into quarters lengthwise, then cut each quarter into smaller pieces suitable for juicing.
  2. Prepare the apples. Wash apples thoroughly. Remove the core and seeds by slicing around the center core, leaving a seedless apple pillar to discard. Slice the remaining apple flesh lengthwise, then rotate and make small, thin cuts. This helps the juicer process the apples more easily.
  3. Start juicing the base fruits. Turn on the juicer and begin by running a small handful of grapes through, followed by apple pieces. Alternate between grapes and apples to help prevent clogging and keep juice flowing smoothly.
  4. Prepare and juice the oranges. Peel oranges completely, removing as much of the peel as possible to avoid bitterness. Cut each orange into smaller segments. Juice them using apple or blackberries to help push the softer fruit through the juicer.
  5. Add ginger and berries. Chop ginger into very small pieces to protect the juicer. Add blackberries whole or chopped. Continue alternating with apple pieces as needed.
  6. Prepare and juice the banana. Cut bananas lengthwise, then quarter with a widthwise cut, and chop into small pieces. Add banana pieces to the juicer. Banana adds creaminess and softens the sharpness of citrus.
  7. Finish with apples and grapes. Run remaining apple pieces and grapes through last. This helps clear the juicer and makes cleanup easier.
  8. Serve. Pour juice into a container and enjoy immediately. Stir gently if needed. The final juice should be smooth, slightly thick, balanced, and refreshing.

Ehret's Honey Lemonade, JC Version

A lighter, quicker-to-make stand-in for fruit juice.

Watch the full walkthrough

This recipe has its own video in Transition Diet 101, Module 5, under Menu Plan 2. The video will be watchable there. Coming soon.

Transition Diet 101 →
Menu plan
Plan 2Plant-based, still lots of starchSee facing page
Meal
Breakfast
Timing
2 hoursbefore a fruit or vegetable meal

Ingredients

  • 2 lemons (produces about 100 ml juice)
  • 1 lime (produces about 50 ml of juice)
  • 1 orange (produces about 100 ml of juice)
  • Raw honey, about 8 oz. Some practitioners prefer to use less, often about half this amount, based on how their body negatively responds to higher quantities of honey.
  • 1 cup / 250 ml of the cleanest water available, for dissolving the honey and diluting

Equipment

  • Manual or electric citrus juicer
  • Knife
  • Cutting board
  • Medium-sized pot
  • Spoon (for stirring)
  • Strainer
  • 32-ounce mason jar (or similar sized container)
  1. Prepare the citrus. Wash all citrus. Cut and juice the lemons, lime, and orange using a manual or electric juicer. Periodically clear pulp from the juicer to maximize juice yield. You should end up with about 8 ounces (250 ml) of fresh citrus juice.
  2. Dissolve the honey. Add 1 cup / 250 ml of the cleanest water available to a medium-sized pot. Pour in the honey, coating the base of the pot with a thick layer. Heat on high, then reduce slightly, stirring continuously with a spoon until the honey is fully dissolved. Warm gently, do not boil. The goal is to dissolve the honey, not overheat it.
  3. Combine honey water and citrus. Place a strainer over the pot and pour the citrus juice into the warm honey water. This helps catch excess pulp and remaining seeds, and slightly cools the mixture.
  4. Build the drink. Pour the honey-citrus concentrate into a 32-ounce mason jar. Fill the jar the rest of the way with the cleanest water available.
  5. Stir thoroughly. Stir well before drinking. The honey and citrus are heavier than water and will settle at the bottom if not mixed.
  6. Serve. Enjoy warm or cold. This honey lemonade can be used in place of fruit juice for a lighter, quicker-to-make option.

Ingredients

  • Raw cashews, peanuts, or pumpkin seeds, ¼ cup
  • Clean water, enough to boil them plus extra for blending. Start with water up to their level in the blender
  • Fresh dill, ½ cup
  • Fresh ginger root, ½ to 1 teaspoon, finely chopped. Start smaller if unsure
  • Garlic, 2 to 3 large cloves. Use 1 if you are not baking or cooking the final dish
  • 50 ml lemon juice plus 50 ml lime juice, about 100 mL total combined
  • Honey, about 1 tablespoon, plus more to round out sharpness if needed
  • Green salt (salicornia powder), about 1 tablespoon, optional
  • White salicornia salt, about ¼ teaspoon, optional

Equipment

  • Small pot
  • Stove
  • Measuring cup (¼ cup)
  • Strainer (fine mesh preferred)
  • Blender (Magic Bullet or similar)
  • Citrus juicer (manual or electric)
  • Knife and cutting board
  • Spoon (for tasting)
  1. Boil the nuts or seeds. Add ¼ cup cashews, peanuts, or pumpkin seeds to a small pot with about 2 inches of water. Boil on medium-high to high for about 5 minutes until very soft. This improves smoothness.
  2. Prep the blender cup. While they boil, add to the blender cup: ½ cup dill, torn or broken up for easier blending; ½ to 1 tsp ginger, finely chopped; 2 to 3 garlic cloves, lightly chopped; about 100 mL lemon plus lime juice; 1 tbsp honey; and optionally 1 tbsp green salt plus ¼ tsp white salicornia salt.

  • Water rule: start low. If you over-thin it, it is difficult to recover without turning it into a whole mess of extra ingredients.
